How the pipeline works.

Three design choices distinguish this from a conventional multi-sensor health model. Each is implemented in code you can inspect.

1
Temporal coverage gate

A feature is admitted only if its record actually spans the study window. A variable present for part of the period would otherwise be imputed for the rest, and any importance it received would be partly an artefact of the fill. Every admission and exclusion is written to an auditable report.

2
Leakage blocklist

The candidate bank contains columns that are themselves functions of mortality. Including any of them would let the model predict life expectancy from life expectancy. They are blocked before modelling, and the block count is printed in every run log.

3
County-grouped cross-validation

All years of any county fall in a single fold, so every reported number describes prediction of counties never seen in training. Median imputation is fitted on the training fold only. There is no global imputation anywhere.

4
Protected feature list

Redundancy filtering never removes a physiologically motivated variable. Without this, a composite index built from its own inputs would be deleted automatically and its importance could never be tested.

5
Learner tournament, then interventional attribution

Several gradient-boosted families compete under the identical protocol, and attribution runs on the winner. The interventional estimator credits a feature only with what it adds beyond its co-variates, which is what makes the ranking interpretable under heavy collinearity.
